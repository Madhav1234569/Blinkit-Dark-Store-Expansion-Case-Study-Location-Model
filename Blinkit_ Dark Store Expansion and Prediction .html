Root cause analysis

Why dark store forecasting fails

Five structural issues make a macro-driven forecast unreliable for dark stores.

1 · The flaw of "macro" data

A common shortcut ranks cities by income, population and density, then assumes small cities will be weak. Blinkit's own data contradicts most of those assumptions. Management says income matters, but less than people expect.

Assumption in the marketWhat Blinkit's data showsImplication for the model
Incomes are lower in small citiesTrue, but matters less than expectedGive income a low weight. Use observed catchment behaviour.
Baskets are smallerContradicted. NAOV is 85–90% of metro levels, with premium beverages, branded diapers and gaming consoles in baskets.Do not scale metro basket priors down by a fixed income ratio.
Density is too thinContradicted. Cities contributing about 96% of NOV are contribution-positive.Learn density thresholds from data, not from assumptions.
Retention is weakerContradicted. Retention is comparable to metros.Share cohort retention curves across city tiers.

2 · Latent demand

Management calls small-city demand "chronically underserved." Products that are unavailable locally never appear in sales history, so a model fit on past sales underestimates potential. Add proxies: app searches for unstocked items, out-of-zone orders and add-to-cart abandonment.

3 · Capacity, not demand

A store has a finite throughput, so orders per day reflect capacity as much as demand. Low-margin discount orders use the same picker and rider capacity as regular orders. Forecasting raw demand overstates what a store can fulfil.

4 · Structural breaks

The shift to own inventory (about 80% of quick commerce NOV in Q2FY26, target about 90%) changed what revenue means. Adjusted Revenue grew 76% reported and 58% like-for-like. The Q2FY26 GST cut caused a purchase delay, and festive builds swing working capital. Model these as explicit regressors.

5 · Metric definitions

GOV overstates growth because discounts are funded by restaurants and brands. In food delivery, Q2FY26 GOV growth was 18% against 14% NOV growth. In quick commerce, AOV rose 5% while NAOV fell 1%, with opposite conclusions. Forecast NOV or NAOV, never GOV or AOV.

Root cause in one line: the forecast is built on city macro proxies, supply-censored sales history, unlabelled structural breaks, and targets that mix discounts with demand.

Target variables

  • Orders per day at day 30, 60 and 90, benchmarked against the 1,000-a-day level management cites.
  • NAOV and category mix, not GOV.
  • Months to contribution breakeven per store.
  • Store-level Adjusted EBITDA trajectory. Mature cities already run above 5%.
  • Quarterly net additions by city and cluster, tested against guidance.

Validation and pitfalls

  • Validate by opening cohort and held-out city, not random rows. Random splits leak future store information.
  • Include failures. Eternal wrote down ₹8 crore on Zomato Local Services after closing its hyperlocal operations. A model trained only on surviving stores is overconfident.
  • Keep the operational-day definition of NOV per store per day consistent.
  • Mark structural breaks from the 1P shift, GST change and festive build.
  • Forecast hierarchically (store, cluster, city, national) and reconcile to guidance.
  • Report intervals, not points. Capex decisions depend on the downside case.

Productivity check

NOV per store per day rose from ₹7.71 lakh (Q2FY26) to ₹9.02 lakh (Q2FY27) while the network grew about 51%. New stores have not diluted productivity so far. The forecast should test whether that holds in smaller and newer-category markets, where ramp and mix are less proven.

Key takeaways

Business lessons

1 · Measure NOV, not GOV

Discounts inflate GOV. Forecast on net order value and NAOV.

2 · Let the catchment lead

Blinkit's small-city data contradicts most macro assumptions.

3 · Plan against the pipeline

At 308 net adds a quarter, the March 2027 target is out of reach. Accuracy on new-store ramp matters most.

4 · Learn from closures

Failed operations are the best negative examples a model can get.
